# Мужской трек — вопросы для OpenEvidence

> 2026-09-15. Материалы Perry Academy — только про женщин. В базе знаний воркера по мужчинам есть
> отдельные строки (силовые и тестостерон, сон), но нет структуры направлений тренировок.
> Порядок как в прошлых раундах: владелец задаёт вопросы в OpenEvidence, ответы кладёт в
> `interpreter/Infa Cloude/M/` (по файлу на вопрос, номер в имени), Claude сжимает в evidence-документ.
> Задавать по-английски (ниже), по одному вопросу.

## A. Основа: что тренировать мужчине 40–65

1. In men aged 40–65 with declining testosterone (andropause / late-onset hypogonadism symptoms), what exercise
   modalities (resistance training, HIIT, aerobic, combined) have RCT or meta-analysis evidence for improving
   muscle mass, strength, body composition and symptoms? Give weekly frequency, volume, intensity and duration.
2. What is the evidence that resistance training increases basal (not acute) testosterone in middle-aged and older
   men? Distinguish acute post-exercise rises from long-term changes.
3. Optimal resistance training prescription for preventing sarcopenia in men 40–70: sets, reps, load (%1RM),
   frequency, progression; heavy vs moderate loads.
4. Evidence for HIIT vs moderate continuous training in men 40–65 for visceral fat, insulin sensitivity,
   VO2max and blood pressure. Safe HIIT protocols for previously sedentary middle-aged men.

## B. Мужские особенности и ограничения

5. Bone health in men 50+: evidence for resistance and impact exercise on bone mineral density; is jump/impact
   training recommended for men as for women?
6. Pelvic floor muscle training in men: evidence for urinary symptoms (post-void dribbling, urgency), erectile
   function, and after prostatectomy. Can pelvic floor work be safely included in general group workouts?
7. Exercise and erectile dysfunction: which modalities, dose and duration have evidence for improvement?
8. Exercise in men with benign prostatic hyperplasia / lower urinary tract symptoms: benefits, and any
   activities to avoid (e.g., prolonged cycling)?
9. Cardiovascular safety of vigorous exercise in men 40–65 who start from sedentary: warning signs, when
   pre-exercise medical screening is needed, how to progress intensity safely.
10. Exercise prescription for men with abdominal obesity, hypertension or type 2 diabetes — what to prioritize
    and what to modify.
11. Joints in middle-aged men (knee osteoarthritis, low back pain, shoulder problems): how to adapt resistance
    training without stopping it.

## C. Восстановление и данные

12. Recovery in men 40–65: how many hours between resistance sessions for the same muscle group; does age
    change recovery needs; role of sleep.
13. Evidence for using HRV and resting heart rate from wearables to adjust daily training intensity
    (HRV-guided training) in middle-aged recreational exercisers.
14. Sleep restriction or obstructive sleep apnea and exercise in men: effect on testosterone and on exercise
    tolerance; should training be modified?

## D. Практика и удержание

15. Adherence to home-based resistance training programs in men 40–65: what formats, session lengths and
    progressions keep men training for 12+ weeks?
16. Minimum effective dose: how little resistance training per week maintains strength and muscle in men 50+?

## E. Уточнения — конкретные протоколы (2026-09-15)

17. Describe in detail the exercise protocol of the Kukuljan/Daly 18-month RCT in men aged 50–79
    (progressive resistance training plus weight-bearing impact exercise, with calcium–vitamin D fortified milk):
    list of exercises, sets, repetitions, load and its progression, impact exercises (type, number of impacts,
    heights, progression), session length, weekly frequency, warm-up/cool-down, and safety modifications.
18. Describe the LIFTMOR-M trial protocol (high-intensity resistance and impact training in men with low bone mass):
    exercises, sets/reps, loading and progression, jumping chin-ups/impact components, supervision, adverse events.
19. How can a supervised combined resistance + impact program for men 50–79 be adapted for home use without
    a gym (dumbbells, bodyweight, bands, step): which substitutions keep the bone and muscle stimulus, and what
    are the safety limits (vertebral fractures, knee/back pain)?
