# P-F10 Когнитивный туман в менопаузе — углубление: вопросы для OpenEvidence (15)

Восьмой узел, завершает «мозговой» кластер: настроение (P-F9) → сон (P-F17) → энергия (P-F5) → познание.
Особенность: «туман» пугает («это ранняя деменция?»), а по факту у большинства он лёгкий, преходящий и
проходит после менопаузы — нужен деликатный НЕ-пугающий, но и не отмахивающийся фрейминг. Сильные
двойники (депрессия, сон/апноэ, щитовидка, B12/железо, лекарства, алкоголь) и рынок ноотропов-пустышек
(гинкго, ежовик/Lion's Mane, фосфатидилсерин). Поэтому 15 вопросов: +дифференциал, +отличие от MCI/деменции,
+честная карта добавок.

Цель: ИИ должен (1) РАСПОЗНАВАТЬ менопаузальный когнитивный паттерн по нашим сигналам, (2) УСПОКОИТЬ без
обесценивания (лёгкий/преходящий, ≠ деменция), (3) НЕ выдавать туман за «мозг сдаёт», когда за ним стоит
сон/настроение/щитовидка/B12/лекарства, (4) дать доказательные рычаги, (5) честно про добавки, (6) красные
флаги истинной патологии → к врачу.

## Что уже есть
- **Клинический KB** P-F10 (~L95): MIND-диета, DHA, холин 425 мг (эстроген участвует в продукции холина,
  веганки в зоне риска), фосфатидилсерин, Lion's Mane, B12/фолат, магний-треонат, COSMOS-мультивитамин
  (↓темп когнитивного старения); «туман = окно кардиометаб. профилактики (сердце↔мозг)».
- **Велнес-KB** P-F10 (~L741): одна строка (омега-3 DHA, рыба, яйца/холин, ягоды; фосфатидилсерин,
  Lion's Mane, B12/фолат; сон ≥7 ч) — нет менопаузальной рамки, УСПОКОЕНИЯ, дифференциала, отличия от
  деменции, честной оценки добавок, красных флагов.
- Данные до ИИ доходят: ясность/туман, память (симптом), сон/глубокий, настроение, тревога, приливы,
  энергия, WHtR/кардиометаб. Лаб (ТТГ/B12) НЕТ → критичен дифференциал и перенаправление.

## Вопросы для OpenEvidence (English)

### A. Physiology & menopause-specificity
1. What is menopausal "brain fog" physiologically — how does estrogen decline affect cognition (hippocampal/prefrontal function, cerebral glucose metabolism, cholinergic system, BDNF) — and, crucially, what is the honest prognosis: is it typically mild, transient, and does it resolve after menopause? How to reassure without dismissing, and why it is NOT early dementia in most women?
2. Which specific cognitive domains actually change across the menopause transition (verbal memory/learning, processing speed, attention/working memory) vs which are preserved, what is the magnitude (subtle, usually within normal range), and the perimenopausal dip → postmenopausal recovery trajectory?
3. How much of menopausal cognitive complaint is a direct hormonal effect vs downstream of vasomotor symptoms, sleep disruption, mood, and cortisol (P-F17/P-F9/P-F4) — quantified — and how do these interact as a hub?

### B. Recognition & differential diagnosis
4. What everyday/consumer signals track menopausal cognitive change (subjective fog/memory complaints, sleep metrics, mood, VMS burden, cardiometabolic markers), and what is the relationship between SUBJECTIVE cognitive complaints and OBJECTIVE performance (do worriers actually perform worse; is subjective complaint driven by mood/anxiety)?
5. **DIFFERENTIAL — mood & sleep:** How do depression/anxiety ("pseudo-dementia") and sleep disorders / obstructive sleep apnea produce cognitive complaints that mimic menopausal fog, and what flags these as the primary driver?
6. **DIFFERENTIAL — medical & iatrogenic:** How do hypothyroidism, B12/folate deficiency, iron-deficiency anemia, vitamin D, and medications (anticholinergics, sedatives/benzodiazepines, antihistamines, alcohol) cause cognitive complaints in midlife women — and the phenomenon of ADHD becoming apparent in midlife women as estrogen falls?
7. **DIFFERENTIAL — MCI / early dementia:** How to distinguish benign menopausal fog from mild cognitive impairment or early dementia — which features flag true pathology (functional decline, getting lost in familiar places, misplacing items in odd locations, progressive word-finding/language loss beyond tip-of-tongue, poor judgment, personality change, informant/family concern, young-onset) → cognitive evaluation?

### C. Solutions — levers with magnitudes
8. **Exercise** (aerobic + resistance) for cognition and BDNF in midlife/menopausal women — effect sizes on memory, executive function, processing speed; dosing.
9. **Diet** — MIND/Mediterranean pattern, omega-3 DHA, dietary choline, flavonoids/berries — effect sizes for cognition; and the **COSMOS multivitamin trial** evidence for slowing cognitive aging.
10. **Sleep, stress reduction, and treating vasomotor symptoms** as cognitive levers — quantified effects (cross-link P-F17/P-F4/P-F9).
11. **Cognitive reserve & modifiable dementia risk factors** (cognitive engagement, education/mental activity, hearing loss correction, social engagement, cardiometabolic risk control — the "brain fog as prevention window", alcohol reduction) — effect sizes per the Lancet Commission on dementia prevention.
12. **Cognitive supplements/nootropics** — ginkgo biloba, Lion's Mane (Hericium), phosphatidylserine, omega-3, B-vitamins, "nootropic" stacks — what human RCT evidence actually shows for cognition vs marketing hype, effect sizes, safety, and the wellness/App-Store caution on dementia-prevention/"brain-boosting" claims.

### D. Safety / communication (non-diagnostic wellness)
13. For a consumer wellness (non-diagnostic) app: how to describe menopausal cognitive change **without diagnosing MCI/dementia AND without dismissing** — reassuring honestly (common, usually benign/transient) while flagging when evaluation is warranted; avoiding both false alarm and false reassurance.
14. **Red flags** for cognitive referral — functional impairment (managing finances, medications, getting lost), safety concerns (driving), rapid/progressive decline, focal neurological symptoms, informant-corroborated decline, young-onset — mapped to which clinician/evaluation?
15. **Long-term brain-health framing:** how should a wellness tool honestly discuss the menopause transition as a **prevention window** for long-term cognitive/dementia risk (cardiometabolic control, hearing, exercise, sleep) WITHOUT catastrophizing or implying inevitability — and where does hormone therapy's cognitive evidence honestly sit (not for cognitive indication)?
