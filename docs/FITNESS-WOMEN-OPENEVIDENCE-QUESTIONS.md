# Женский трек «Движение 40+» — вопросы для OpenEvidence

> 2026-10-06. К утверждениям Т1–Т29 и Н1–Н6 из [`FITNESS-WOMEN-EVIDENCE.md`](FITNESS-WOMEN-EVIDENCE.md).

> `interpreter/Infa Cloude/FIZ/` файлом **`Q<номер>.txt`** (`Q5.txt`). ⚠️ Уже лежащий там `1.txt` — это
> ответ про белок (Н1), не вопрос №1; его не переименовываем.

> **Порядок:** сначала А и Е (без них нельзя запускать: нагрузка и безопасность), потом Г, Б, В, Д, Ж.
> Каждый вопрос просит числа и уровень доказательности — без них ответ не превращается в правило.

Общая приписка, которая уже вшита в каждый вопрос: *women aged 40–65 in perimenopause or postmenopause;
give numbers (frequency, sets, reps, %1RM or RPE, duration, thresholds), state the level of evidence
(meta-analysis / RCT / cohort / expert consensus), and note contraindications.*

## А. Сила — основа (Т1–Т5)

1. For women aged 40–65 in perimenopause or postmenopause, what resistance training prescription has the best
   RCT or meta-analysis evidence for muscle mass, strength, physical function and body composition: weekly
   frequency, number of exercises, sets, repetitions, load (%1RM or RPE/RIR), rest intervals, and whether training
   to failure is needed? Compare 2 vs 3 sessions per week. State the level of evidence and contraindications.

2. What is the minimum effective dose of resistance training to maintain strength and muscle mass in women 45–65
   (sessions per week, sets per exercise), and how quickly are gains lost after 2–4 weeks without training?
   Give numbers and level of evidence.

3. How should load be progressed in home-based resistance training for previously untrained women 40–65
   (double progression, "2-for-2" rule, RPE-based progression)? How often should difficulty increase, and what
   signs mean progression is too fast? Give numbers and level of evidence.

4. In women 40–65, how many hours of recovery are needed between resistance sessions for the same muscle group,
   and is it safe for beginners to do strength training on two consecutive days if different muscle groups are
   trained? Does menopause change recovery needs? Give numbers and level of evidence.

## Б. Кости, прыжки, интервалы (Т6–Т10)

5. What impact (jump) exercise dose improves or preserves hip and spine bone mineral density in perimenopausal
   and postmenopausal women: type of jumps, number of impacts per session, sessions per week, height, rest
   between jumps, progression and minimum duration of the program? Is impact more effective combined with
   resistance training? Give numbers, level of evidence and contraindications.

6. In women with stress urinary incontinence or pelvic organ prolapse symptoms, is jumping/impact exercise safe?
   How many sessions of pelvic floor muscle training (with breathing coordination) should precede impact work,
   and which signs mean impact should be replaced by a low-impact alternative? Give numbers and level of evidence.

7. Describe the LIFTMOR trial protocol (high-intensity resistance and impact training in postmenopausal women with
   low bone mass): exercises, sets/reps, %1RM, jumping chin-ups and drop landings, progression, supervision and
   adverse events. Can a similar program be done safely at home without supervision using dumbbells or bodyweight,
   and what must be changed? Level of evidence.

8. For perimenopausal and postmenopausal women, what HIIT or sprint interval protocols have RCT evidence for
   VO2max, visceral fat, insulin sensitivity, blood pressure and menopausal symptoms: interval length, intensity,
   number of rounds, sessions per week? Compare with moderate continuous training. Give numbers and level of
   evidence.

9. Safety of HIIT in women 40–65 who are new to vigorous exercise: who should not do HIIT (hypertension,
   arrhythmia, cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis), warning signs during a session, and how to progress safely?
   Level of evidence.

10. Is there evidence that moderate-intensity continuous aerobic exercise ("zone 2", "grey zone") is less effective
    than HIIT or resistance training for women in perimenopause? What does moderate cardio contribute (blood
    pressure, mood, sleep, cardiovascular risk), and how much per week? Level of evidence.

## В. Корпус, тазовое дно, баланс, мобильность (Т11–Т13)

11. Pelvic floor muscle training in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women for stress urinary incontinence and
    prolapse symptoms: effective dose (contractions, holds, sessions per week, program length), and can it be
    integrated into general group or video workouts rather than done separately? Level of evidence.

12. Core training for women 40–65 with low back pain or diastasis: which exercises and breathing strategies are
    supported, and which should be avoided (e.g., crunches, breath-holding)? Level of evidence.

13. What balance and agility training dose reduces falls and fall risk in women aged 50–70 and in women with
    osteoporosis: hours per week, types of exercise, program length? Give numbers and level of evidence.

14. For joint stiffness in women 40–65 (knee/hip osteoarthritis, stiff shoulders), is dynamic rhythmic mobility
    (e.g., cat–cow, hip circles) better than static stretching as a warm-up before resistance training and on
    low-energy days? Level of evidence.

## Г. Готовность дня (Т14–Т18)

15. What is the evidence for using wearable HRV (rMSSD), resting heart rate and sleep duration, compared with a
    personal baseline, to adjust daily exercise intensity in recreationally active middle-aged women? What
    deviation from baseline should lower intensity, how many days of data are needed to build a baseline, and does
    HRV-guided training outperform a fixed plan? Give numbers and level of evidence.

16. How do poor sleep and night sweats/hot flushes the previous night affect strength performance, perceived
    exertion and injury risk in women 40–65? Should training intensity or type be changed after a bad night?
    Level of evidence.

17. Delayed-onset muscle soreness versus pain warning signs in women 40–65 doing resistance training: when is it
    safe to train with soreness, which pain signs require stopping, and when should a person see a doctor?
    Level of evidence.

18. In perimenopausal women with irregular cycles, does menstrual cycle phase meaningfully affect strength,
    performance or injury risk? Should training be adjusted for cycle phase or only for symptoms (heavy bleeding,
    pain, fatigue)? Summarize McNulty 2020 and Colenso-Semple 2023/2025 and any perimenopause-specific data.

## Д. Жалоба выбирает тренировку (Т19–Т22)

19. Which exercise types reduce hot flushes and night sweats in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women
    (resistance training such as Berin 2019, aerobic, yoga)? Give the dose, the size of the effect and how long it
    lasts. Also: which exercise improves mood and anxiety in menopause? Level of evidence.

20. Does evening or high-intensity exercise worsen sleep in women in perimenopause with insomnia? What exercise
    type, timing and dose improve sleep quality in this group? Level of evidence.

21. For abdominal (visceral) fat in postmenopausal women, compare resistance training, HIIT, moderate aerobic
    exercise and combined training: dose and expected change. Level of evidence.

22. How should resistance and impact training be adapted (not stopped) for women 40–65 with knee osteoarthritis,
    low back pain or shoulder problems: exercise substitutions, range-of-motion limits, load? Level of evidence.

## Е. Безопасность до первой тренировки (Т23–Т27)

23. What pre-participation screening is recommended before a home resistance and impact exercise program for
    women 40–65 (ACSM 2015 algorithm, PAR-Q+): which answers mean "start freely", "start with modifications"
    and "see a doctor first"? Give the exact questions and decision rules usable in an app.

24. Is self-reported effort (RPE, "easy / moderate / hard", reps in reserve) valid and safe to auto-regulate
    training load in women 40–65 without heart-rate monitoring? How many sessions rated "easy" justify moving up a
    level, and how many "hard" ones should move down? Level of evidence.

25. Exercise in women 40–65 with hypertension or arrhythmia: home blood pressure values at which exercise that
    day should be skipped or limited to mobility; breath-holding/Valsalva and isometric holds; whether HIIT is
    contraindicated. Give the thresholds and level of evidence.

26. Exercise in women with osteoporosis or a history of vertebral fracture: which movements to avoid (loaded spinal
    flexion, twisting), maximum loads, whether impact is allowed, and safe alternatives (consensus such as
    "Too Fit To Fracture"). Level of evidence.

## Ж. Удержание (Т28–Т29)

27. Adherence to home-based video-led exercise programs in women 40–65: which session length (20–25 vs 40 min),
    weekly frequency, instructor-led video vs written plans, and progression features keep women training for
    12+ weeks? Level of evidence.

## З. Питание вокруг тренировок (Н2–Н6)

28. In women 40–65 doing resistance training, does protein timing around the session (pre/post within 1–2 h)
    add benefit for muscle mass and strength when total daily protein (1.0–1.6 g/kg) is already met? Give numbers
    and level of evidence.

29. How should perimenopausal and postmenopausal women combine resistance training with a calorie deficit to lose
    fat while preserving muscle and bone: safe size of deficit, minimum energy intake, rate of weight loss, protein
    target, and signs of low energy availability? Level of evidence.

30. Creatine monohydrate supplementation in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women combined with resistance
    training: effects on muscle, strength, bone and mood; dose, duration, safety and who should avoid it.
    Level of evidence.

31. Dietary fat and omega-3 intake in women 40–65 doing resistance training: does omega-3 (EPA/DHA) enhance muscle
    strength or mass response (e.g., Smith 2015), and what total fat intake supports hormonal and cardiovascular
    health in midlife? Give amounts and level of evidence.

32. Hydration and thermoregulation during exercise in women with hot flushes: does exercise in heat or high
    intensity trigger hot flushes, and what practical measures (cooling, timing, fluid intake) are supported?
    Level of evidence.
