# A walk after meals: 10 minutes that change the whole day

*3 min read*

After lunch you feel sleepy, a couple of hours later you crave something sweet, and your belly grows even though you eat "as usual". One of the simplest remedies is a short walk right after eating.

## Why it works

After a meal, blood sugar rises. When muscles move, they take up sugar — a separate route for absorbing it, on top of the usual one. The peak comes out lower, which means the dip after it is smaller too.

A sedentary day in itself makes it harder for the body to handle sugar. So it helps not only to walk after meals, but also to get up for at least a couple of minutes every 15–20 minutes of sitting.

Walking works especially well together with the step "Vegetables and protein before carbs": the effects add up.

## How to do it

- **10–15 minutes of walking after main meals.** Best right after eating, not an hour later.
- **A normal pace** — you can talk. It's not a workout, just movement.
- **At your desk — short breaks:** stand up, walk around, take the stairs.

## Who should be more careful

- **Diabetes medication:** with a new load, blood sugar can drop more than usual. Keep a quick snack at hand, and stop if you feel weak or shaky.
- **Injury, illness, fever** — get better first; the step can wait.

## What can get in the way

- Desk work. A walk after lunch, calls on the move, stairs instead of the lift.
- Weather. Walking around the house or a shopping centre counts too.
- Evening. Even 10 minutes after dinner is better than going straight to the sofa.

## What to expect

Watch your after-lunch sleepiness and sweet cravings, and after a few weeks — your waistline. Log how you feel in VIA-L.
