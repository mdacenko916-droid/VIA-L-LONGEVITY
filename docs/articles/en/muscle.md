# Muscles after 40: why they weaken and how to keep them

*4 min read*

It is harder to lift a bag, harder to get up from a low sofa, and climbing the stairs leaves you out of breath. Muscle quietly wastes away with age — and in the transition this speeds up. The good news: muscle responds at any age, and getting your strength back is realistic.

## What is happening

Oestrogen helps muscles, and when there is less of it, muscle responds less well to both protein and exercise. Put simply, after 40 you need **more** of both to get the same result.

Why this matters beyond your figure:

- muscle is the main "consumer" of sugar, and the more of it you have, the easier it is for your metabolism;
- muscle is an "energy factory": with it, you feel less tired;
- weakness leads to falls, and falls to fractures.

## What works

**Strength training 2–3 times a week.** This is the main thing. The basics are squats, pulls and presses, without pain. The load should be noticeable: the last repetitions take effort. With a trainer, results and safety are better.

**Protein — more than you think.** Aim for 30–40 g of protein at every meal, starting with breakfast: that is usually where there is least. That is, for example, a portion of cottage cheese or Greek yoghurt with eggs, a piece of fish or chicken, a plate of lentils with tofu. Over the day, aim for at least 1–1.2 g of protein per kilogram of body weight, and more if you do strength training.

**Movement every day.** Walking, stairs, housework — in addition to strength training, not instead of it.

**Balance.** Balance exercises protect against falls.

## What about supplements?

- **Protein powder** is convenient if you can't get enough protein from food. It is food, not medicine.
- **Creatine** is best studied together with strength training: a small but real gain in strength and muscle, including in postmenopausal women. Without training, the data are mixed. With kidney disease — only after talking to a doctor. Creatinine in blood tests rises slightly while taking it — this is not kidney damage.
- **Collagen** does not help muscles.

## When to see a doctor

- Weakness is increasing quickly, without a clear reason.
- You have had a fall or a fracture from a minor injury.
- It is hard to get up from a chair without using your hands, when you used to manage.

## What to try this week

- Two strength sessions of 30–40 minutes on different days.
- A source of protein at every meal — start with breakfast.
- At least once a day, take the stairs instead of the lift.
