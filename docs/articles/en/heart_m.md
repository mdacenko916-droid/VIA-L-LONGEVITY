# A man's heart after 40: what really protects it

*5 min read*

Heart and blood vessel disease is the leading cause of death in men. It sounds frightening, but there's good news in it: the risk is driven by a few things you can largely control. Not supplements and not your watch's readings, but a handful of "boring" levers.

## What matters most

Nine factors you can influence account for more than 90% of the risk of a first heart attack: blood pressure, smoking, cholesterol, diabetes and belly fat, inactivity, diet, alcohol, stress. The gap between these levers and trendy "optimisations" is huge.

## The levers — by strength

**Fitness.** The most underrated lever. Even a small improvement in stamina noticeably lowers mortality — comparable to quitting smoking. The biggest jump comes when you go from "barely moving" to "moderately fit". A guide: 150 minutes of aerobic exercise a week and two strength sessions.

**Don't smoke.** The fastest lever. Even one cigarette a day carries about half the excess risk of a pack — "cutting down" doesn't work, only quitting does. A year after quitting, the risk of heart disease halves. E-cigarettes are probably less harmful, but not harmless.

**Blood pressure.** The single biggest risk factor. Measure it at home with a validated upper-arm cuff.

**A Mediterranean style.** Proven in large trials: about 30% fewer heart events. Olive oil as the main fat, a handful of nuts a day, legumes, fish, vegetables. Salt — under 5 g a day, less ultra-processed food.

**Fish, not fish oil.** Oily fish twice a week is good for you. Over-the-counter omega-3 capsules haven't shown a benefit for the heart, and high doses raise the risk of rhythm problems.

**Belly and sleep.** Fat around the waist and sleep apnea raise blood pressure and the risk of rhythm problems.

**Alcohol.** "A glass of wine is good for the heart" — it didn't hold up. Don't start for your heart's sake, and if you drink — drink less.

## Know your numbers

What your watch can't see, you find out from a doctor: blood pressure, cholesterol and, if possible, **ApoB**, **Lp(a)** — once in a lifetime (it's inherited and raised in about one in five people), blood sugar. At intermediate risk, a doctor may suggest a coronary artery calcium scan.

## What about watches and rings?

An irregular rhythm notification is the most useful thing a watch can do: it's worth showing a doctor. But no notification doesn't rule out rhythm problems. Wrist blood pressure hasn't been validated yet — re-measure with an upper-arm cuff. And most importantly: a "perfect" dashboard can't see plaque in your arteries.

## "Heart" supplements

No supplement has been shown to reduce heart attacks. In a head-to-head comparison, popular supplements didn't lower cholesterol, while a low dose of a statin lowered it noticeably. **Red yeast rice** is essentially a statin with no dose control and possible contaminants. Niacin showed no benefit in large trials, but there was harm. "Artery cleansing" and "dissolving plaque" have no data in humans.

## About statins — honestly

We don't prescribe them. They're among the most studied medicines. Most "side effects" in blinded studies happened just as often on placebo. The decision is made with a doctor based on your personal risk. Fear and stopping them on your own raise the risk of heart attack and stroke.

## When it's urgent

- Pressing, squeezing or burning pain behind the breastbone, especially on exertion, spreading to the arm, jaw or neck, with breathlessness, sweating or nausea — at rest, this is a reason to call an ambulance.
- Fainting during exertion.
- New or worsening breathlessness.
- Calf pain when walking that goes away at rest.
- New erection problems — also a reason to get your heart checked.

## What to try this week

- Three brisk 30-minute walks.
- Measure your blood pressure at home morning and evening, two or three days in a row.
- Two portions of oily fish this week.
