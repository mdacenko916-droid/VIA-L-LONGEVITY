# Thoughts about sleep: how to stop forcing yourself to fall asleep

*4 min read*

Sleep can't be switched on by willpower. The harder you try to fall asleep, the more tension in your body and head — and the further away sleep gets. That's why CBT-I works with thoughts separately.

## Familiar thoughts

- "If I don't fall asleep tonight, I won't be able to work properly tomorrow."
- "If I only get four hours again, it will ruin my health."
- "I have to fall asleep right now, or tomorrow everything will go wrong."

They sound like facts, but they're forecasts — and usually far too gloomy.

## Four questions for an anxious thought

- What evidence supports this thought — and what goes against it?
- How realistic is the worst-case scenario? What actually happened after past bad nights?
- Would I cope if it did happen?
- What would I tell someone close to me if they were in the same situation?

The goal isn't to "think positive" but to ease the pressure of "I must fall asleep no matter what". As soon as that pressure eases, sleep comes more easily.

## Put worries off until tomorrow

During the day or early evening, set aside 10–15 minutes: write down what's worrying you and, for each item, one next step. When the thought comes back at night, you can tell yourself: "It's written down already; I'll deal with it tomorrow." The app has a button for this — "Offload my thoughts".

## Mindfulness — 5 minutes during the day

Mindfulness teaches you to notice thoughts without getting pulled into them and to gently bring your attention back to your breath or body. Practise for 5 minutes every day, during the day — not only at night when you already can't sleep. Gradually it becomes easier not to cling to anxious thoughts, not to spin out scenarios and not to try to control every detail of sleep.

The goal isn't to stop the stream of thoughts, but to stop reacting to each one as a threat.

## Be kinder to yourself

Not every night has to be perfect. One bad night is one bad night, not the start of a disaster. The body knows how to catch up on sleep by itself.
