# The prostate after 40: what's normal, what's treatable and when to see a doctor

*5 min read*

You have to get up at night to pee. The stream is weaker, and afterwards it feels like you haven't fully emptied. The worrying thought — "what if it's cancer?". Almost always it's something else — common, benign and very treatable.

## What's happening

With age, the prostate gets bigger in almost all men. This is benign enlargement (BPH), and on its own it isn't a disease. It needs treatment when the symptoms bother you, get in the way of life or threaten the bladder and kidneys.

Symptoms come in two kinds:

- **storage** — often, urgently, getting up at night;
- **voiding** — a weak stream, straining, a stop-start flow, not emptying fully.

## It's not cancer

Urinary symptoms almost always come from benign enlargement, while early prostate cancer usually **causes no symptoms**. So "having symptoms" doesn't mean "cancer", and "no symptoms" doesn't mean "safe". Benign enlargement doesn't raise the risk of cancer.

## Getting up at night — not always the prostate

If you get up more than twice a night, and you also snore and feel sleepy during the day, the cause may be sleep apnea. More in the article "Sleep after 40".

## What works — about as strong as medicines

The strength is in combining the steps, not in any single one:

- drink less in the evening and before bed;
- less coffee — it makes urgency and frequency worse;
- an evening without alcohol;
- go to the toilet on a schedule, "double voiding" — wait and try once more;
- pelvic floor exercises.

**Your waist.** Extra fat around the waist and a poor response to sugar push the prostate to grow. Reducing your waist is the most powerful goal.

## What about "prostate" supplements?

- **Saw palmetto** is the best-selling supplement, but in rigorous studies it's no better than a placebo.
- **Beta-sitosterol, pygeum, pumpkin seed oil, nettle** — old, small studies that haven't been repeated.
- **Vitamin E** raised the risk of prostate cancer in the largest trial. **Selenium** has no benefit and is harmful for those who already get enough. **High doses of zinc** over a long time are linked to dangerous forms of cancer.

"Prostate complexes" are often made of exactly these. The main danger is lost time before real help.

## What a doctor can offer

There are tablets that quickly ease urination and tablets that shrink the prostate. The second kind has two important points: they lower PSA by about half (your doctor needs to take this into account), and they can lower mood — tell your doctor straight away if that happens. There's one medicine that helps both the prostate and erections, but it must not be combined with nitrates. You choose with a urologist, based on your priorities.

## PSA — a personal decision

PSA is tested after a conversation with a doctor: there's a benefit, but a small one, and plenty of unnecessary biopsies and worry. PSA is also raised by causes that aren't cancer: an enlarged prostate, inflammation, recent ejaculation. If prostate cancer runs in your family or you're of African descent — discuss it earlier.

## Be careful with cold medicines

"Blocked nose" remedies with pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine and "night-time" tablets with diphenhydramine can cause urinary retention. Newer allergy medicines are safer.

## When to see a doctor — urgently

- You can't pee — this is an emergency.
- Blood in the urine, even once.
- Repeated urinary tract infections.
- Pain in the bones or back together with weight loss.
- Numbness in the groin or weakness in the legs.
- Pain in the groin, pelvis or during ejaculation — this isn't an enlarged prostate and needs a separate assessment.

## What to try this week

- Your last big glass of liquid — 2–3 hours before bed.
- Coffee only in the morning, an evening without alcohol.
- A few minutes of pelvic floor exercises every day.
