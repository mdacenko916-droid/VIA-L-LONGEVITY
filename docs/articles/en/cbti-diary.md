# Sleep diary: why you need one and why it beats a tracker

*3 min read*

The sleep diary is the main tool of CBT-I. A few lines every morning, and after a week you see the real picture instead of the feeling "I don't sleep at all".

## What to write down

- when you went to bed;
- roughly how long it took to fall asleep;
- how many times you woke up and for how long;
- when you finally woke up and got out of bed;
- how long you slept in total;
- daytime naps;
- coffee, strong tea and alcohol.

Everything roughly. Don't look at the clock at night: that alone wakes you up and makes you anxious.

## Sleep efficiency

The diary gives the key number of CBT-I — **sleep efficiency**: how much of your time in bed you actually slept. Eight hours in bed, six asleep — 75% efficiency. The target is 85% or higher. If it's lower, sleep is "spread thin" across the night, and a sleep window helps (see the separate article).

In the app, when the Sleep block is on, the diary questions appear in the Sleep step of the morning check-in, and efficiency is calculated for you.

## What about watches and rings?

They're good for seeing the trend: how much sleep in total, roughly when you fall asleep. But the moment you fall asleep, short awakenings and especially sleep stages ("deep", "REM") they measure imprecisely. They can't replace a diary or a doctor's assessment.

There's a flip side too. When people judge the night only by numbers on a screen and chase perfect scores, anxiety grows and sleep gets worse. There's even a word for it — **orthosomnia**. If your tracker's numbers worry you, look at them once a week rather than every morning.

## What to try

- Fill in the diary every morning for two weeks in a row — just a couple of lines.
- Look at the weekly average, not at a single night.
