# Brain fog in men after 40: what causes it and what helps

*4 min read*

You forget why you walked into a room. A thought slips away mid-conversation. Focusing on one task is harder than it used to be. The worrying thought — "is this the start of something?". Almost always the cause is something else, and fixable.

## What's normal and what isn't

A small slowdown after 45 is normal: speed of thinking and memory dip a little, while your store of knowledge grows. Dementia at 40–65 is rare. Normal is when you remember later and your life isn't affected. A signal is when you forget important things you used to remember, the people close to you notice, and it gets in the way of daily life.

## What most often lies behind the fog

**Sleep and apnea.** The most powerful and most underrated cause. In deep sleep the brain "rinses itself", and short sleep and apnea break that. More in the article "Sleep after 40".

**Mood.** In men, depression often looks exactly like fog, irritability and loss of interest — without sadness. Check your mood first, before blaming age or testosterone.

**Stress** — through sleep and mood.

**Medicines.** The "cheapest" and often missed lever. Over-the-counter "night-time" products with diphenhydramine, older antihistamines, sleeping pills, sedatives and opioid painkillers dull memory and attention. Don't stop them on your own — discuss it with a doctor.

**Alcohol.** Even one or two drinks a day are linked to a smaller brain volume, and memory and attention are worse the next day too. The good news: recovery starts within a few weeks without alcohol.

## What helps the brain

**Movement.** The strongest lifestyle lever: aerobic and strength exercise lower the risk of dementia by about 20–28% and bring clarity even after a single workout.

**Learning something new and challenging.** A language, an instrument, a new craft. "Brain training" apps don't carry over into real life.

**Hearing.** One of the main risk factors for dementia that you can change. If you've started asking people to repeat themselves or turning the TV up — get your hearing checked.

**Food.** A Mediterranean style, oily fish twice a week, less ultra-processed food.

**The shingles vaccine.** It's already recommended after 50, and in observational studies dementia was less common after it. Decide with a doctor.

## What about supplements and "smart pills"?

No over-the-counter memory supplement has solid evidence in healthy people. Ginkgo doesn't protect and raises the risk of bleeding with blood thinners. "Stacks" of many ingredients are the worst option. Stimulants "for focus" raise blood pressure, wreck sleep and cause dependence. In trials, testosterone didn't improve memory.

## When to see a doctor without delay

- Worsening memory loss that gets in the way of life: bills, driving, familiar routes.
- A change in personality or behaviour: apathy, disinhibition, loss of empathy.
- Rapid decline over weeks or months.
- Weakness, vision problems, tremor or a change in how you walk.
- Walking, memory and frequent urination problems together.

## What to try this week

- Get up at the same time all seven days — sleep first.
- A week without alcohol.
- Two 30–40 minute workouts on different days.
