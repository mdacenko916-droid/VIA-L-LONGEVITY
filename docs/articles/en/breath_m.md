# Slow breathing: calm in five minutes

*3 min read*

Tension doesn't let go all day, irritation flares up over small things, and in the evening your head keeps "working". Slow breathing is one of the simplest ways to calm the nervous system. It works without willpower and without special conditions.

## Why it works

A slow inhale and especially a long exhale switch the nervous system from "alarm" mode to "rest" mode. It's a reflex — you don't have to talk yourself into it. About six breaths a minute for just five minutes raise heart rate variability and lower stress hormones.

Behavioural methods — breathing, mindfulness, cognitive behavioural therapy — teach the stress system to calm down on its own and are stronger than "anti-stress" supplements. "Adaptogens" is a marketing word, not a class of medicines.

## How to do it

- **Breathe in through your nose for a count of four, breathe out slowly for six.** That comes to about six breaths a minute.
- **5–10 minutes a day.** Even five minutes has an effect.
- **In any pause:** before a meeting, in the car before getting out, after work, in the evening before bed.
- **When irritation or anxiety hits** — switch straight to slow, long exhales.

Shoulders relaxed, breathe with your belly. If you feel dizzy — breathe more shallowly and a little faster.

## Who should be more careful

**With asthma**, breathe calmly, without holding your breath or straining. If an attack starts, stop and use your usual medication.

## What can get in the way

- "My mind wanders". That's part of the practice: notice it — come back to counting.
- "I forget". Tie the breathing to something that happens every day anyway: the car in the car park, brushing your teeth, switching off the light.
- "Breathing alone won't fix my sleep". True: for sleep, a fixed wake-up time and the "bed is for sleep only" rule are stronger. Breathing is an addition to them.

## What to expect

Watch how often tension and irritation hit. If you have a ring or a watch, you can compare heart rate variability on days with and without the breathing — it's a recovery signal, not a hormone measurement. Log how you feel in VIA-L.

## If it doesn't help

If low mood, irritability or anxiety last more than two weeks and get in the way of life — that's a reason to see a doctor. More in the article "Irritability, apathy, 'nothing brings joy'".
