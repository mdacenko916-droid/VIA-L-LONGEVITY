# Sleep window: less time in bed, deeper sleep

*4 min read*

When sleep is poor, it's tempting to go to bed early and lie in longer in the morning. But that only breaks sleep up more: the more time you spend in bed awake, the weaker your natural drive to sleep at night. A sleep window (in the clinic it's called sleep restriction) does the opposite — it matches time in bed to what you actually need.

## How it works

- **A fixed wake-up time.** Choose a wake-up time and keep it all seven days, however the night went.
- **Time in bed ≈ actual sleep.** If your diary shows 8 hours in bed but 5.5–6 hours of sleep, the window is narrowed closer to your real sleep: go to bed later, get up at the same time.
- **Widening in steps.** When weekly sleep efficiency is 85% or higher, the window is widened by 15 minutes. If it's below 80%, it's narrowed a little. Decide by the week's diary, not by one night.

In the first week you may feel sleepy during the day — that's expected and passes. Usually within 2–4 weeks sleep becomes more solid and falling asleep takes less time.

## Important: don't cut your sleep on your own

A sleep window doesn't mean depriving yourself of sleep. In the clinic the window is never set shorter than 5 hours and is adjusted under a specialist's supervision. On your own, the gentler version — **sleep compression** — makes more sense: shorten time in bed gradually, by 15–30 minutes a week, and not below 6 hours.

Don't narrow the window without a doctor if:

- you feel very sleepy during the day, especially when driving or working with dangerous machinery;
- you snore with pauses in breathing — check for apnea first;
- you work nights or shifts;
- you have had epileptic seizures or bipolar disorder.

## Together with the bed rule

The window works hand in hand with the rule "the bed is for sleep only". Can't sleep for more than about 20 minutes? Get up, spend some time in another room in dim light and come back when you start to feel sleepy. Go to bed when you're sleepy, not when it's "time".
