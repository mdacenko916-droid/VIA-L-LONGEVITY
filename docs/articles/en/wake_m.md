# Same wake-up time and morning light: an anchor for the whole day

*3 min read*

It seems as if sleep starts in the evening. In fact it starts in the morning — with what time you get up and see light. It is one of the strongest and most underrated levers for sleep, energy and mood, and it costs nothing.

## Why it works

**Regularity matters more than duration.** According to the data, a steady time for getting up and going to bed matters no less than how many hours you sleep — and it is strongly linked to heart health. Weekend "catch-up sleep" until noon throws the internal clock off again.

**Short sleep hits more than alertness.** Short and irregular sleep raises stress hormones, lowers testosterone and adds fat specifically around the belly.

**Morning light is a strong signal to the clock.** 10–15 minutes outdoors work even on an overcast day. And daylight also supports mood.

## How to do it

- **Get up at the same time all seven days**, weekends included, with no more than half an hour of variation. The goal is 7–9 hours of sleep.
- **In the first hour after getting up — 10–15 minutes of daylight.** A walk, coffee on the balcony, walking to work.
- **In the evening — the opposite:** dim light and no screens 30–60 minutes before bed.

## What can get in the way

- A bad night. Even after one, get up at your usual time — that way the next night will be better.
- A dark winter morning. Go outside as soon as it gets light, or spend the first minutes by a window.
- Weekends. You can allow yourself half an hour, but not three hours.

## What to expect

The clock doesn't reset in one night. Judge over a week or two: how you fall asleep, how you wake up, how much energy you have during the day. Log your sleep and how you feel in VIA-L.

## When to see a doctor

If sleep still isn't refreshing on a regular schedule, if there is loud snoring with pauses, or if you fall asleep during the day — discuss a sleep study with a doctor. More in the article "Sleep after 40".
