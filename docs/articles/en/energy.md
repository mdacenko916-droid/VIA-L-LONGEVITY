# Where tiredness after 40 comes from and how to get your energy back

*5 min read*

You seem to be sleeping, you seem to be eating as usual, yet you have no energy from the moment you wake up. By lunchtime you want to lie down; by evening you don't want anything. The first thought is "I'm low on vitamins". But most often, vitamins are not the issue.

## What most often lies behind tiredness

Falling oestrogen really does affect stamina. But tiredness at this age far more often comes from something else — and almost all of it can be checked and put right:

- **Sleep.** Broken nights add up, and no amount of coffee makes up for them.
- **Iron stores.** Heavy periods in the transition drain your reserves, and tiredness appears long before a routine blood test changes.
- **Thyroid.** What sets it apart is feeling cold — in menopause it is usually the opposite, you feel hot.
- **Mood.** When nothing has brought you joy for more than two weeks, it is not "just tiredness".

So the place to start is not with "energy" supplements but with checking.

## Signs that call for a test, not "pushing through"

- **Iron:** heavy periods, breathlessness or a racing heart on the stairs, hair loss, pallor, craving to chew ice, restless legs in the evening → ferritin and a blood count.
- **Thyroid:** feeling cold, constipation, a slow pulse, dry skin, puffy eyelids, a hoarse voice → TSH.
- **Vitamin B12:** numbness or tingling in the hands and feet, "cotton-wool" legs; the risk is higher with metformin, heartburn medication and a vegan diet → a B12 test.
- **Breathing during sleep:** sleep is unrefreshing despite enough hours, snoring with pauses → see a sleep specialist.

⚠️ A special case: if, after exertion, you feel sharply worse the next day — for hours or a whole day — do not force yourself to do more. See a doctor about this.

## What brings energy back

**Movement.** The strongest energy lever we know of. A combination works best: strength training, short intense bursts with full rest between them, and light cardio. The worst is "moderately hard all the time". The good news: in the first years after menopause the body is especially responsive to training.

**Protein in the morning.** A protein breakfast within an hour or two of waking. A skipped breakfast and sugar spikes themselves drive up stress hormones — and a slump follows during the day.

**A walk after meals.** 10–15 minutes after your main meals smooths out the "sugar rollercoaster" and the after-lunch drowsiness.

**A sleep anchor.** Get up at the same time all seven days and get daylight in the morning. Sometimes the simplest thing is to go to bed half an hour earlier.

## What to remove

Alcohol, refined sweets and ultra-processed food, chronic lack of sleep, intense training without recovery — removing these "energy leaks" matters more than adding something.

## What about supplements?

Food and movement are stronger than any of them. **Iron** — only based on a ferritin test, not blindly. **Vitamin D** is fine even without a test. Magnesium and B vitamins add energy only if you are short of them. Trendy "youth boosters" (NMN, NR and the like) did not improve energy in human studies — we do not recommend them.

## When to see a doctor

- Tiredness lasting more than four weeks despite sleep and movement.
- Tiredness together with unexplained weight loss, drenching night sweats or swollen lymph nodes.
- Breathlessness, chest pain or palpitations — urgently.

## What to try this week

- A source of protein at every meal, starting with breakfast.
- 10–15 minutes of walking after your main meals.
- Two strength sessions of 30–40 minutes on different days.
