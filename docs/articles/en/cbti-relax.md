# Relaxing before bed: a helper, not a treatment

*3 min read*

With insomnia, the body often stays "switched on" at night: muscles tense, pulse fast, mind busy. Relaxation techniques lower that tension and make the move into sleep easier.

## What CBT-I uses

- **Slow breathing.** Breathe in for a count of 4, out for 6. A long out-breath calms the pulse. Five minutes is enough.
- **Progressive muscle relaxation.** One group at a time — hands, shoulders, face, belly, legs — tense the muscles for 5 seconds and let go, noticing the difference.
- **Body scan.** Slowly move your attention from the top of your head to your feet, changing nothing, just noticing.
- **A calm image.** Picture a place where you feel calm, in every detail: sounds, smells, warmth.

## Honestly, how much it helps

On its own, relaxation usually doesn't cure chronic insomnia. It works as part of CBT-I — together with a fixed wake-up time, a sleep window and working with thoughts. Don't expect a practice to "switch you off" in five minutes: its job is to lower tension, and sleep will come by itself. If you fall asleep during the practice — good; if you don't — that's fine too.

## Music and sounds

Slow, calm music without drums (60–80 beats per minute) modestly improves sleep in studies, especially when you listen regularly for several weeks. There's little evidence for noise (white or pink), and in one study pink noise played all night even made sleep worse. That's why the app's ritual plays a quiet melody that fades after the practice, rather than a sound that runs all night.

## How it works in the app

The wind-down ritual (5, 10 or 15 minutes) in the "My evening ritual" step guides you by voice through breathing, muscle relaxation and a body scan. If you can't sleep at night, there's a short "Can't sleep" practice.

The voice in the ritual is your phone's built-in voice. You can change it or download a more natural one in your phone's settings.
