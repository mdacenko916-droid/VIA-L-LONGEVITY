# Why hormone tests after 40 tell you so little

*4 min read*

When you start to feel different, the first instinct is to "get your hormones checked" and see the answer in numbers. But in the transition years, these are exactly the tests that let you down most often. Let's look at why — and which tests are really worth doing.

## Why hormones are hard to catch with a test

**They jump around.** In perimenopause, oestrogen swings up and down, and FSH, oestradiol and progesterone change so much after 40 that one test is a snapshot of one random day. Tomorrow the numbers will be different.

**Progesterone pulses.** Its blood level can change several-fold within minutes. Even a blood test shows just a moment, and home tests do not measure it at all.

**Your gadget does not measure hormones.** A ring or watch sees the rise in temperature after ovulation — but only after the fact and only as a marker. If there was no rise, that does not mean "low progesterone": after 40 some cycles happen without ovulation, and sensor noise, a hot flash or a cold can also get in the way.

That is why perimenopause is recognised not by a test but by the picture over time: how your cycle, sleep, mood and hot flashes change. A diary kept for a few weeks says more than one lab report.

## What is important to rule out

Some transition symptoms resemble other conditions that are easy to check and can be dealt with. These are worth ruling out first:

- **Thyroid** — if you have become sensitive to cold, constipated, or have a slow pulse: a TSH test.
- **Iron stores** — if your periods are heavy, you feel tired, get breathless on the stairs, crave chewing ice, or your legs are restless in the evening: a ferritin test. Iron falls before a routine blood test changes.
- **Mood** — if low mood or anxiety lasts the whole month, not just before your period, that is a reason to talk to your doctor separately.
- **Breathing during sleep** — snoring with pauses, unrefreshing sleep, daytime sleepiness: after menopause this is a common and often missed cause of tiredness.

## Which tests really help after 40

- **Blood pressure and sugar** — regularly.
- **ApoB** — shows the particles that are dangerous for blood vessels more precisely than ordinary "bad cholesterol".
- **Lp(a)** — one test in a lifetime is enough: it is inherited and rises after menopause.
- **hs-CRP** — a marker of background inflammation; in women it predicts heart risk well.
- **Ferritin and TSH** — if the signs above apply.
- **Bone density scan** — already at menopause if you have risk factors: menopause before 45, a fracture from a minor injury, long-term steroid use, a parent with a hip fracture.

Ask your doctor for more than just "cholesterol" — if possible, ApoB and Lp(a).
