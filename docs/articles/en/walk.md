# A walk after meals: 10 minutes that change the whole day

*3 min read*

After lunch you feel sleepy, a couple of hours later you crave something sweet, and by evening you have no energy. This is often not "character" but the sugar rollercoaster after meals. And one of the simplest remedies is a short walk straight after eating.

## Why it works

After a meal, blood sugar rises. When muscles move, they take up sugar — this is a separate route for using it, in addition to the usual one. The peak is lower, and so the slump that follows is smaller too: less drowsiness and fewer sugar cravings.

Walking works especially well together with another step — "Vegetables and protein before carbs": the effects add up.

## How to do it

- **10–15 minutes of walking after your main meals.** Ideally straight after eating, not an hour later.
- **At a normal pace,** one where you can still talk. This is not a workout, just movement.
- **If you can't go out** — walk around the house, use the stairs, tidy up, wash the dishes standing.

## Who should take more care

- **Diabetes medication:** with new exercise, blood sugar can drop more than usual. Keep a quick snack to hand, and stop if you feel weak or shaky.
- **An injury, illness or fever** — recover first; this step can wait.

## What can get in the way

- A desk job. A short walk after lunch, taking a call on the move, the stairs instead of the lift.
- The weather. Walking around the house or a shopping centre counts too.
- The evening. Even 10 minutes after dinner is better than going straight to the sofa.

## What to expect

Notice your after-lunch drowsiness and sugar cravings. Log your energy and how you feel in VIA-L.

## Who this step helps

Those with energy slumps after meals, sugar cravings and a growing waist.
