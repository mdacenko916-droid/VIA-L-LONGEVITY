# Bed is for sleep only: how to help your body relearn falling asleep

*3 min read*

You go to bed tired — and sleep disappears. You lie there, scroll your phone, count how long is left until the alarm. The longer this goes on, the more firmly the brain links the bed not with sleep but with being awake and anxious. This step restores the right link.

## Why it works

This is the core of behavioural therapy for insomnia — according to studies it is stronger and longer-lasting than sleeping pills. The idea is simple: the bed should once again mean sleep. When you only sleep in it, the brain relearns how to fall asleep as soon as your head touches the pillow.

## How to do it

- **Don't lie awake.** If you can't sleep for more than 15–20 minutes — get up, go somewhere else with dim light, do something calm. Come back when you start feeling sleepy.
- **Don't work or scroll your phone in bed.** Phone — on the charger, away from the bed.
- **Don't look at the clock at night.** Counting the hours left only adds anxiety.
- **Get up at the same time.** Going to bed early "just in case" often means lying awake longer.
- **No screens 30–60 minutes before bed.**

## Who should be more careful

**If you take blood pressure medication or are over 70:** don't get up abruptly at night — first sit on the edge of the bed for a minute. Keep the hallway light dim, but bright enough not to trip.

If daytime sleepiness gets in the way of driving — don't drive when you're tired.

## What to avoid

**Sleeping pills "for insomnia" until apnea has been ruled out.** Insomnia and apnea often go together, and sleeping pills relax the throat and deepen the drops in oxygen.

## What to expect

Watch how long you lie awake and how many times you wake up. Log your sleep in VIA-L to see the difference week by week.

## When to see a doctor

If poor sleep lasts more than three months despite these steps, or if there is loud snoring with pauses, unrefreshing sleep or falling asleep during the day — it's worth discussing a sleep study with a doctor. More in the article "Sleep after 40".
