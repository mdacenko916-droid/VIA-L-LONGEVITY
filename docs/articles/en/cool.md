# A cool bedroom: a simple way to sleep better with hot flashes

*3 min read*

At night you are thrown into a heat, you kick off the duvet, then you get cold, then hot again — and so on until morning. If this sounds familiar, a cool bedroom is one of the simplest steps you can take today.

## Why it works

In the transition the "thermal comfort zone" narrows: a very small amount of heat is enough to trigger a hot flash. And most night-time hot flashes coincide with waking up. A cool bedroom keeps your body further from that threshold.

To be honest: cooling does not restore the comfort zone and does not "cancel" hot flashes. But it noticeably reduces night-time awakenings and discomfort — and adds deep sleep.

## How to do it

- **Bedroom temperature around 16–19 °C.** Air the room before bed, turn the heating down.
- **Darkness.** Blackout curtains or a sleep mask.
- **Breathable fabrics.** Bedding and nightwear in cotton, linen or other breathable fabrics.
- **Layers instead of one warm duvet.** A light duvet plus a throw — so you can easily take something off at night without fully waking.
- **A cooling mattress topper or pillow** — if your hot flashes are severe.
- **Water by the bed** and a spare T-shirt — in case of night sweats.

## What can get in the way

- Your partner gets cold. Separate duvets help: one each.
- A hot summer. A fan, airing the room at night, a cool shower before bed.
- The habit of warm pyjamas. Switch to light ones, and keep warm socks — if your feet get cold.

## What to expect

Look first of all at how many times you wake in the night. Log your sleep and hot flashes in VIA-L to compare the week before and the week after.

## If it doesn't help

If severe hot flashes still break up the night, that is a reason to see a doctor. Treating the hot flashes themselves improves sleep too. More in the article "Hot flashes: why they happen and what really helps".

## Who this step helps

Those with night-time hot flashes, night sweats and waking in the night.
