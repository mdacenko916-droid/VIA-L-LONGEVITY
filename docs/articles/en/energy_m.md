# No energy after 40: where men's fatigue comes from and what brings it back

*5 min read*

You wake up already tired, get through the morning on coffee, and by evening only have the energy for the couch. The first thing that comes to mind is "age", "the adrenals" or "testosterone". In reality, fatigue almost always has specific causes you can fix.

## "Adrenal fatigue" is a myth

There's no such diagnosis: endocrinologists have rejected it, and there are no valid tests for it. "Adrenal support" supplements don't help and sometimes contain hidden hormones. The main harm of the myth is that it takes time away from the real cause.

The fatigue itself is real. You just need to look for it somewhere else.

## What most often lies behind fatigue

- **Sleep and apnea.** Broken sleep and breathing pauses at night are a common and almost always undiagnosed cause. More in the article "Sleep after 40".
- **Mood.** In men, depression often looks not like sadness but like "no energy", irritability and loss of interest.
- **Belly and metabolism.** Fat around the waist and sugar spikes steal your energy on their own.
- **Testosterone.** It drops with short sleep, stress and extra weight — and comes back when the cause goes away.
- **Medicines and alcohol.** An evening "drink to relax" makes sleep and recovery worse.

Thyroid disease is rare in men, so "it's all the thyroid" is more the exception than the rule. A doctor checks it with a blood test, not by symptoms.

## Signs that call for a test, not "pushing through"

- **Vitamin B12:** numbness or tingling in the hands and feet; the risk is higher with metformin, heartburn medicines and a vegan diet.
- **Anaemia:** paleness, breathlessness on the stairs. Iron deficiency in a man is never normal — it's always a reason to get checked.
- **Breathing during sleep:** loud snoring with pauses, sleep that doesn't refresh.

## What brings your energy back

**Movement.** For a man over 40, the risk of moving too little is far higher than the risk of "overdoing it". Regular training lowers resting stress hormones, and an easy walk lowers them straight away.

**Sleep and its regularity.** Get up at the same time all seven days, 7 or more hours. Short sleep raises evening stress hormones and lowers testosterone.

**An evening without alcohol.** It delays the drop in stress hormones and cuts into the second half of the night. One of the most rewarding free steps.

**Coffee in the morning.** Daytime coffee keeps pushing stress hormones up. Moving your coffee earlier pays off more than taking "anti-stress" supplements.

**Slow breathing.** About six breaths a minute for five minutes — it eases tension within minutes.

## What about supplements?

"Adaptogens" is a marketing word, not a group of medicines. We don't recommend ashwagandha: the effect is modest and the risks for the liver and the thyroid are real. Magnesium makes sense only if you get too little from food. "Testosterone boosters" don't treat fatigue.

## When to see a doctor

- Rapid weight gain together with muscle weakness, purple stretch marks, easy bruising.
- Darker skin, dizziness when standing up, salt cravings, unexplained weight loss, nausea.
- Unexplained weight loss or anaemia.
- Breathlessness, chest pain or palpitations — urgently.

## What to try this week

- Get up at the same time all seven days.
- Last coffee before lunch, an evening without alcohol.
- A 10–15 minute walk after your main meals.
