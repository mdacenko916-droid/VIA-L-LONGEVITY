# Your cycle after 35: heavier, less frequent, less predictable — what is happening

*5 min read*

Your period comes after three weeks, then disappears for two months. It has become heavier and longer, with clots. A pad doesn't last the night. Between 35 and 50 such changes are very common — they are one of the first signs of the transition. But "common" does not mean "put up with it".

## Why your cycle changes

In the transition, more and more cycles happen without ovulation, and there is less progesterone. Oestrogen, meanwhile, does not fall — it swings, sometimes higher than usual. Hence the unpredictability and heavier periods.

If you wear a ring or watch: they see the rise in night-time temperature after ovulation only after the fact. If there was no rise in a cycle, that does not mean "low progesterone" — after 40 some cycles simply happen without ovulation.

## What counts as heavy

Heavy periods are often thought of as "just how I am" and go unchecked for years. Guides:

- you need to change a pad or tampon **every hour for several hours in a row**;
- clots larger than a coin;
- leaks, needing "double protection";
- periods lasting longer than 7 days;
- bleeding limits your normal day.

## Heavy periods and iron

Heavy periods are the most common cause of iron deficiency in women. Iron stores fall before a routine blood test changes, and tiredness, breathlessness on the stairs, hair loss, brittle nails and feeling cold appear already then. Particular clues: a craving to chew ice and restless legs in the evening.

**What to do:**

- **Ask for a ferritin test** — it is the main measure of iron stores. Iron should be taken based on a test, not blindly: too much iron is harmful too.
- **Iron-rich food.** Iron from meat, poultry, fish and seafood is absorbed best. Plant sources — lentils, beans, tofu, pumpkin seeds, dark leafy greens — are best combined with vitamin C at the same meal: bell peppers, citrus fruit, kiwi.
- **Tea, coffee and dairy — separately** from iron-rich food, about an hour apart: they interfere with its absorption.

And most importantly: heavy periods can be treated. Gynaecologists have options that reduce blood loss several-fold. Iron without addressing the cause is a patch, not a solution.

## Keeping track is half the job

Log the first day of your period in VIA-L. After a few cycles you will see how your cycle length is changing, and it is easier to go to the doctor with this. A diary also helps you see how your cycle is linked to sleep, mood and hot flashes.

## What you don't need

- **Supplement sets "by cycle phase"** are marketing: taking supplements by phase is no better than daily.
- **"Seed cycling"** — there are no clinical data. Seeds are good as food, but rotating them by day changes nothing.

## When to see a doctor without delay

- You need to change a pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row.
- Periods lasting longer than 8 days.
- Bleeding between periods or after intimacy.
- Bleeding together with severe tiredness, breathlessness, feeling faint or fainting.
- **Any bleeding after 12 months without periods** — always a reason to see a gynaecologist promptly.
