**BPH is driven primarily by DHT acting on prostate stromal cells, and the estradiol-to-testosterone ratio — elevated by visceral adipose aromatase activity — synergistically promotes prostate proliferation.** The 2026 AUA Guideline on LUTS/BPH (Part I) and the AAFP rapid evidence review provide the current framework for evaluation and management.[1][2]

## 1. NUTRITION

**Mediterranean dietary pattern** has the strongest overall evidence, with a 2025 study demonstrating improved urinary health in men with BPH.[3] **Lycopene** (cooked tomato products with olive oil) showed PSA reduction and prevention of prostate enlargement in a pilot RCT, though systematic review evidence is inconclusive.[4][5] **Cruciferous vegetables** provide DIM, a competitive inhibitor of DHT binding to the androgen receptor that modulates estrogen metabolism toward protective 2-hydroxyestrogen metabolites.[6][7] **Zinc-rich foods** (pumpkin seeds, oysters, legumes) support prostate structure and function.[8][9] Limit evening fluid intake to reduce nocturia.[1][10]

## 2. NUTRACEUTICALS

- **Saw palmetto: NOT recommended** — Cochrane review and JAMA RCT (up to 960 mg/day) showed no benefit over placebo[11][12]
- **Beta-sitosterol (60-130 mg/day):** Best phytotherapy evidence — RCT showed significant IPSS improvement (-7.4 vs. -2.1), improved peak flow, and reduced residual volume[13][14][15]
- **Pygeum africanum (100-200 mg/day):** Cochrane review of 18 RCTs supports moderate benefit[16]
- **DIM (100-200 mg/day):** Strong mechanistic rationale (androgen receptor antagonist, estrogen metabolism modulator, AMPK activator) but no RCTs in BPH[6][7][17][18]
- **Zinc (15-30 mg/day, max 40 mg/day):** Supports prostate function; excess >100 mg/day may increase prostate cancer risk[8][9]
- **Lycopene (15 mg/day):** Adjunctive; pilot data promising but inconclusive[4][5]

## 3. ELIMINATION

- **Caffeine >2 cups/day:** 2.09-fold increased odds of LUTS progression[19]
- **Alcohol:** Worsens LUTS; interacts with alpha blockers causing orthostatic hypotension[1][20]
- **Decongestants (pseudoephedrine):** Contraindicated — can precipitate acute urinary retention[21]
- **Anticholinergics:** Reduce detrusor contractility, worsen retention[21][22]
- **Endocrine disruptors (BPA, phthalates, parabens):** Increase estrogenic load, worsening E2:T imbalance
- **Evening fluids:** Limit 2-3 hours before bedtime[1][10]

## 4. LAB MARKERS

**PSA interpretation:** Not recommended for routine LUTS evaluation; shared decision-making for cancer screening.[1] **Free PSA ratio** [23][24] **PSA density** (PSA/prostate volume) is now a category 1 NCCN recommendation with mpMRI.[23] **PHI score** (AUC 0.74-0.77) outperforms total PSA and percent free PSA in the 2-10 ng/mL range.[25][26][27] Obtain **testosterone, free T, SHBG, estradiol** to assess the hormonal milieu, plus fasting glucose/insulin (insulin resistance promotes prostate growth), lipid panel, and hs-CRP.[28][1]

**Biopsy referral (NCCN 2026):** PSA >3 ng/mL in high-risk individuals; PSA >3 with suspicious DRE in average-risk; mpMRI with high suspicion → image-guided biopsy.[23]

## 5. LIFESTYLE

- **Pelvic floor exercises:** First-line self-management per AAFP and AUA[1][10]
- **Weight loss:** Obesity increases BPH risk across all metrics; each 0.05 increase in waist-to-hip ratio = 10% increased BPH risk[29][30][31]
- **Exercise:** Meta-analysis of 43,083 men showed OR 0.70-0.74 for BPH/LUTS with physical activity vs. sedentary; active men 34% less likely to report severe nocturia[32][33]
- **Stress reduction:** Sympathetic activation increases prostate/bladder neck smooth muscle tone — mindfulness mimics alpha-blocker effects[34][1]

## 6. PRIORITY #1

**Obtain an IPSS score to quantify symptom severity, then implement visceral fat reduction (Mediterranean diet + exercise ≥150 min/week) and behavioral modifications (limiting evening fluids, reducing caffeine, pelvic floor exercises) — while obtaining baseline labs (PSA with shared decision-making, testosterone, estradiol, fasting glucose, lipid panel) to identify hormonal and metabolic drivers.**
