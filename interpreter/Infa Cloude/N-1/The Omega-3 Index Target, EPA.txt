## The Omega-3 Index Target, EPA vs. DHA Dosing, Anti-Inflammatory Mechanisms, and Why "Just Fish Oil" Falls Short

The **target Omega-3 Index (O3I) is 8–11%** (EPA + DHA as a percentage of erythrocyte membrane fatty acids), a range associated with **~30% lower risk of fatal CHD** and **15–18% lower all-cause mortality** compared to an O3I [1][2][3] Achieving this target typically requires **≥1,300 mg/day of EPA + DHA** in non-fish eaters, or **≥1,100 mg/day supplemental EPA + DHA** combined with ≥2 fish servings/week — doses that most OTC fish oil supplements fail to deliver.[4] EPA and DHA have **distinct but complementary anti-inflammatory roles**: EPA generates E-series resolvins and stabilizes cell membranes, while DHA produces D-series resolvins, protectins, and maresins and more potently raises the O3I — yet both are needed for optimal inflammation resolution.[5][6][7][8]

**4. Why "Just Fish Oil" Often Falls Short**

Multiple converging problems explain why typical OTC fish oil supplements frequently fail to achieve therapeutic omega-3 levels or clinical benefit:[24][6][25][26][27]

**Problem 1: Insufficient EPA + DHA content per serving**

A JAMA Cardiology cross-sectional analysis of 2,819 fish oil supplements found:[27]

- Median daily dose of EPA + DHA was only **600 mg** (IQR 300–1,100 mg)
- Only **9.4%** of supplements contained ≥2 g/day EPA + DHA
- A typical "1,000 mg fish oil" capsule contains only **~300 mg EPA + DHA** — the rest is other fatty acids
- At 300 mg/day, achieving an O3I of 8% from a baseline of 5% is mathematically impossible[15]

**Problem 3: Bioavailability differences by formulation**

The chemical form of EPA + DHA significantly affects absorption:[15][29][30][31]

- **Bioavailability hierarchy:** Free fatty acids (NEFA) > Phospholipids (PL) > Re-esterified triglycerides (rTG) > Natural triglycerides (TG) > **Ethyl esters (EE)** (lowest)[30]
- Most inexpensive OTC supplements use **ethyl ester** formulations — the least bioavailable form
- EE formulations require **pancreatic lipase** for hydrolysis before absorption; this is impaired in fasting states, with low-fat meals, and in patients on PPIs or with pancreatic insufficiency[29][31]
- TG-based supplements raise the O3I by **~1 percentage point more** than EE products at the same dose[15]
- Monoglyceride (MAG) formulations showed **~2× higher** 24-hour plasma AUC for EPA compared to EE[31]

**Problem 4: The "1 g fish oil" misconception**

The ACC 2021 Expert Consensus Pathway explicitly states: **"Nonprescription fish oil products have not been demonstrated to have cardiovascular outcomes benefits and are not recommended for ASCVD risk reduction."** The distinction is critical:[25]

- A "1,000 mg fish oil capsule" ≠ 1,000 mg EPA + DHA
- Most contain only 300–500 mg EPA + DHA per capsule
- To achieve 2 g/day EPA + DHA from a typical supplement, **4–7 capsules/day** would be needed
- 73.9% of fish oil supplements make health claims, but only 19.2% use FDA-approved qualified health claims[27]

The mineral oil placebo in REDUCE-IT raised LDL-C by ~11% and increased CRP, potentially inflating the apparent benefit of icosapent ethyl.[35][34] The Doi et al. cohort study mimicking both trial designs estimated that the comparator oil differences could explain part — but not all — of the discrepancy, with an **unexplained additional ~13% risk reduction** in REDUCE-IT likely attributable to EPA-specific, triglyceride-independent effects.[34]

Proposed EPA-specific mechanisms beyond TG lowering include: membrane stabilization (vs. DHA's fluidity-increasing effect), inhibition of cholesterol crystalline domain formation, greater antioxidant activity against sdLDL oxidation, and more potent COX-2 gene suppression.[6]

The fundamental reason "just fish oil" falls short is a convergence of **insufficient dose, poor bioavailability, oxidative degradation, and variable content** — a typical OTC capsule delivers only ~300 mg EPA + DHA in the least bioavailable form, while achieving the cardioprotective O3I of 8% requires ~1,300 mg/day of high-quality, non-oxidized EPA + DHA in a bioavailable formulation. Treating the O3I as a measurable, goal-directed biomarker — analogous to LDL-C or HbA1c — transforms omega-3 supplementation from guesswork into precision nutrition.[1][12]
