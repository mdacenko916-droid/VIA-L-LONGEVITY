## Physical Activity and Inflammation: The Acute–Chronic Paradox, Myokines, Exercise Modality, and hs-CRP Reduction

Exercise produces a **paradoxical inflammatory response**: each acute bout triggers a transient proinflammatory surge (primarily IL-6, up to **100-fold** above baseline), yet this very signal initiates an anti-inflammatory cascade that, with chronic training, **lowers resting hs-CRP by ~0.26 SMD (approximately 0.5 mg/L)** and reduces basal IL-6 and TNF-α — even in the absence of weight loss.[1][2][3][4] The key to this paradox lies in the **dual identity of IL-6**: muscle-derived (myokine) IL-6 signals through the **classical pathway** to produce anti-inflammatory and metabolic effects, while adipose-derived IL-6 signals through **trans-signaling** to drive chronic inflammation.[5][4][6]

**Chronic adaptation (with regular training):**

Regular exercise training produces the opposite pattern at rest — **lower basal levels** of all proinflammatory markers:[3][19][20]

- **Resting IL-6:** SMD **−0.16** (95% CI −0.30 to −0.03; P = 0.017) in healthy subjects after long-term training[3]
- **Resting CRP:** SMD **−0.18** (95% CI −0.31 to −0.06; P = 0.005) in healthy subjects[3]
- **Resting TNF-α:** SMD **−0.43** (95% CI −0.62 to −0.24; P [3]

The acute inflammatory response is **attenuated in trained individuals** — the same exercise bout produces a smaller IL-6 spike in trained vs. untrained persons, reflecting improved metabolic efficiency and reduced glycogen depletion.[18][17] This creates a virtuous cycle: each acute bout is less "inflammatory" but still sufficient to trigger the anti-inflammatory cascade.

**Critical proof of concept — the tocilizumab RCT:** Wedell-Neergaard et al. (2019) conducted a landmark randomized placebo-controlled trial in abdominally obese adults: 12 weeks of bicycle exercise reduced visceral adipose tissue mass, but this effect was **completely abolished** when IL-6 signaling was blocked with tocilizumab (anti-IL-6R antibody).[23] This demonstrates that **exercise-induced IL-6 is causally required** for visceral fat reduction — not merely a bystander marker.

**Dose-response of acute IL-6 release:**

The magnitude of the myokine IL-6 response is determined primarily by **exercise duration**, with intensity as a secondary factor:[4]

- **Duration** explains >50% of the variance in post-exercise IL-6 levels; the relationship is exponential with time[4]
- **35 min cycling at 50% VO₂max:** 1.4-fold IL-6 increase; at **69% VO₂max:** 2.7-fold increase (P = 0.04)[4]
- **60 min running at 75% VO₂max:** significantly greater IL-6 than at 55% or 65% VO₂max[24]
- **Marathon running:** ~100-fold increase; **246 km ultra-endurance:** 8,000-fold increase[4]
- **Resistance-based HIIT (30 min):** 48% IL-6 increase, with partial reduction (−20%) within 15 minutes post-exercise[25]
- Even **moderate walking** of sufficient duration can elicit significant myokine responses — "suboptimal exercise conditions" still produce immunoregulatory myokine increases[1]

This Bayesian network meta-analysis (39 RCTs, N = 2,714 postmenopausal women with overweight/obesity) represents the most comprehensive modality comparison to date.[26] The finding that **resistance training is superior for CRP reduction** is supported by additional evidence:

- RT reduced CRP by **32.8%** (P [30]
- RT reduced CRP by a mean difference of **−0.47 mg/dL** (95% CI −0.66 to −0.29; P [31]
- The 2024 AHA Scientific Statement on Resistance Exercise Training confirms RT lowers CRP by **−0.26 to −0.37 mg/L** in adults overall, and by **−2.47 mg/L** in adults with elevated cardiometabolic risk[32]
- A 12-week strength training study in postmenopausal obese women showed a **21.3% CRP reduction** (1.69 → 1.33 mg/L; P [33]

However, a large network meta-analysis of 123 RCTs in overweight/obese individuals found that **aerobic exercise** was most effective for CRP and adiponectin, while **HIIT** was most effective for IL-6 and TNF-α, and RT had the least effect compared to other modalities.[27] This discrepancy likely reflects population differences (general overweight/obese vs. specifically postmenopausal women) and highlights that **the optimal modality depends on the target biomarker and population**.

**Intensity matters — but not linearly:**

A dose-response meta-analysis in postmenopausal obese women (30 RCTs, N = 2,124) found that **moderate-intensity exercise** was the most robust intensity for reducing CRP, IL-6, TNF-α, and leptin across the broadest range of biomarkers.[34][35] Meta-regression showed that the CRP reduction from long-term training was actually **weakened by increasing exercise intensity** — suggesting that moderate intensity sustained over longer periods may be more effective than high intensity for systemic anti-inflammatory adaptation.[3]

**5. The Complete Anti-Inflammatory Cascade of Exercise**

The mechanisms by which regular exercise reduces systemic inflammation operate through multiple converging pathways:[19][20][8]

1. **Myokine-mediated anti-inflammatory signaling:** Each exercise bout releases IL-6 → triggers IL-10 and IL-1ra → suppresses TNF-α and IL-1β → repeated bouts create cumulative anti-inflammatory conditioning[5][19]
2. **Visceral fat reduction:** Exercise-induced IL-6 is **causally required** for visceral fat mobilization (proven by tocilizumab blockade RCT); reduced VAT → reduced portal IL-6 and TNF-α → reduced hepatic CRP synthesis[23][20]
3. **Macrophage phenotype switching:** Exercise inhibits M1 macrophage infiltration into adipose tissue and promotes M2 polarization → reduced local and systemic inflammation[20]
4. **Adiponectin restoration:** Exercise increases adiponectin secretion from adipose tissue → anti-inflammatory, anti-atherogenic, and insulin-sensitizing effects[20][29]
5. **Improved endothelial function:** RT improves flow-mediated dilation by ~2–3% → reduced endothelial activation and adhesion molecule expression[32]
6. **Gut microbiota modulation:** Moderate training restores gut microbiota health in obese individuals → reduced bacterial LPS translocation → reduced systemic endotoxemia[19]

The exercise–inflammation relationship embodies a fundamental biological principle: **transient physiological stress, when repeated regularly, produces systemic resilience**. The acute IL-6 spike from contracting muscle is not a sign of damage but a metabolic and immunoregulatory signal that, through classical signaling, orchestrates fuel mobilization, visceral fat reduction, macrophage reprogramming, and anti-inflammatory cytokine production — ultimately lowering the chronic low-grade inflammation that drives cardiometabolic disease.
